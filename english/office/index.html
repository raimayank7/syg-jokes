Office jokes for work groups

Clean jokes about meetings, emails, Mondays and working from home. Safe for the office group, including the one your manager is in.

  • My boss said, "Think outside the box."
    So I took my lunch box to the park.

  • Our office has only two seasons: "the server is down" and "the AC is too cold".

  • My laptop and I have an agreement.
    It restarts for updates exactly when something is urgent, and I pretend to be surprised.

  • "A quick call" is office language for forty-five minutes.

  • I wrote "team player" on my CV.
    In the interview they asked, "Which team?"
    I said, "Whichever is winning."

  • Monday looked at my to-do list and laughed before I could.

  • The email said, "Please reply at the earliest."
    So I read it at the earliest.

  • My office chair has wheels.
    It is the only thing in my career that moves fast.

  • The tea break is the only meeting that always starts on time.

  • I asked for work from home.
    Now I have home at work: the cooker whistles in every meeting.

  • The printer works perfectly all week.
    It can sense a deadline from two floors away.

  • I replied "Noted" to the email.
    It is office language for "I have seen this, and may we never speak of it again."

  • "Let us take this offline" means the meeting has ended,
    but the problem has not.

  • The form asked, "Where do you see yourself in five years?"
    I wrote, "In this same queue for the microwave."

  • Everyone stays on mute through the whole call.
    Then someone says something important.
    Still on mute.

  • The office group has eighty members.
    When someone asks, "Who will take the notes?", it has zero.

Who this set suits

Work groups, team chats, old colleagues and anyone who has sat through "a quick call". The jokes laugh at meetings, printers, emails and Mondays, which every level of an office shares, so they are safe in a group that includes your manager and the newest trainee.

How to tell them

In an office group, timing is half the joke. The Monday joke belongs to Monday morning, the "quick call" joke to the minute a long call ends, and the tea break joke to four o'clock. Post one, and let it sit; do not explain it or tag people under it. To open a meeting aloud, pick a one-liner, say it plainly, smile and move to the first point within five seconds.

When to hold back

Not during a crisis, a layoff, an appraisal week or right after somebody has been pulled up for a mistake: a deadline joke then sounds like a comment on a colleague. Leave them out of client groups and formal email as well. If the group is strictly for work updates, respect that and keep jokes for the tea table.

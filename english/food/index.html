Food jokes for hungry people

Clean jokes about diets, samosas, tea, weddings and mothers who serve "just a little more". Best read after lunch.

  • My diet started on Monday.
    Nobody informed the samosas.

  • Instant noodles are ready in two minutes.
    These are the same two minutes as "I am leaving in two minutes".

  • My mother's "take a little more" and "a little" are two different measurements.

  • The last piece of barfi in the box belongs to nobody and is watched by everybody.

  • Dipping a biscuit in tea is a sport.
    One second too long, and you are fishing with a spoon.

  • At weddings I am not hungry.
    I am only checking whether the paneer is as good as at the last wedding.

  • The golgappa seller is the only person who serves six customers at once and never loses count.

  • "Just one more roti" is the sweetest lie told at any dining table.

  • The pressure cooker whistles three times.
    Mother hears three. Father hears none.
    I hear "lunch is ready".

  • In mango season, one question echoes through the house:
    "Who counted the mangoes in the fridge?"

  • The fridge has not changed since I looked ten minutes ago.
    I opened it again anyway, in case it had new ideas.

  • Tea solves everything.
    And if it does not, there is a second cup.

  • Four samosas, one small packet of chutney.
    This is the country's oldest unsolved sum.

  • I ordered a salad to be healthy.
    Then I ordered fries so that the salad would not feel lonely.

  • The pickle jar on the top shelf is mother's bank locker,
    with better security.

  • "Dinner is ready" from mother means: come now.
    "Coming" from me means: after this one video.

Who this set suits

Family groups, hostel and flatmate groups, office lunch groups and anybody on the second day of a diet. Food is the safest subject in humour: nobody is hurt by a samosa. Children understand most of these, and grandparents will add their own version.

How to tell them

Send them when people are hungry: just before lunch, at evening tea time, or on the way to a wedding. Aloud, slow down at the name of the food; the listener's mouth should water before the twist comes. The diet jokes are best told about yourself: "my diet started on Monday", never "your diet".

When to hold back

Do not aim a diet or weight joke at any person, and skip them altogether if somebody in the group is unwell, on a strict medical diet or fasting that day. A joke about a full plate also lands badly in the same hour as sad news. Food jokes are for ordinary, comfortable days.
